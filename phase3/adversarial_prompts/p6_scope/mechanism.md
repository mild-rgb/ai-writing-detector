# p6_scope — structure as a consequence of answering less

**Mechanism.** Answer scope, not answer style. The model is told to answer only
the part of the question it would actually know cold, in as much depth as that
part deserves, and to leave the rest alone -- no coverage, no survey of the
options, no "and there are other factors". Length and paragraphing fall out of a
narrower subject.

**Why this one exists.** phase1/NARRATIVE.md §11 and §14 both found the same
thing in the judges' own words: 39 of Haiku's 57 missed-ai items cite a surface
voice marker, and 43 of Sonnet's 59 catches cite *structure or polish*. Sonnet
is not reading vocabulary, it is reading the shape of a complete, balanced,
well-covered answer. Nothing in the other five mechanisms touches that shape at
its source, which is the decision about how much of the question to answer.

**Prediction on record.** The weakest of the six on the surface baselines --
scope does not directly set a line-break rate -- and the strongest against
Sonnet, if the Sonnet stretch check gets run at all. If that pattern shows up it
is worth more than the six prompts, because it says the trivial baselines and
the capable reader are looking at different things, and a prompt can move one
without moving the other.
